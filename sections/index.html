Which sections are earning their floor space

Time spent, shoppers attracted, and how many walk straight past — read together, these say which parts of the shop are working and which are quietly costing you.

Where do customers linger?

Time spent in each section

Typical time for customers who actually stopped. People passing straight through are counted separately.

What to do with it: Long dwell with low sales means the stock is interesting but something blocks the purchase — price, sizes, or staff cover.

How many shoppers reach each section?

Share of visitors who stopped

Out of everyone who came in, the share that stopped in each part of the shop.

What to do with it: A section reached by few customers is a placement problem, not a product problem.

Who stops and who walks straight past?

Stopped versus walked through

A rack people constantly pass without stopping is telling you something.

What to do with it: High traffic with few stops means the display is not catching the eye — change the facing, lighting or price visibility.

Which sections do customers visit together?

If shoppers who browse one section reliably browse another, those two belong near each other — or deliberately far apart, to walk them past everything in between.

Which pairings are strongest?

Sections visited together

Thicker lines mean more shoppers browsed both sections in the same visit.

What to do with it: Move strongly paired sections closer to raise basket size, or further apart to pull traffic through quiet areas.

What order do they shop in?

The route through the shop

Where customers go first, second and third after coming through the door.

What to do with it: Put new arrivals on the first or second step of this route, where attention is highest.
